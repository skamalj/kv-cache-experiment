# salim_javed_v1: the Salim–Javed register (Roman-script Hindustani)

An everyday English sentence becomes the same facts in **original** dialogue with the cadence of 1970s Salim–Javed films:
- short, balanced clauses, often in contrasting pairs;
- pride and calm defiance;
- a one-line aphorism tied to the situation;
- Hindustani diction, in Roman script.

| Split | Rows |
|---|---|
| train | 50 |
| validation | 25 |
| test | 25 |

**Sources are shared with Titan.** The 50 training sources are the same 50 pairs used in the Titan experiments (seed 42, 5 per rhetorical move). Validation and test are 25 sentences each from `titan_v1` validation and test, spread across moves. Every row also carries its `titan_target`, so the two personalities can be compared on identical inputs.

**Written by hand** (Claude), not generated by a script. The rules:
- **Originality:** never actual film dialogue, and no close paraphrase of famous lines. A banned-phrase check runs over every line.
- **The same speaker as the source:** a request stays a request, and a question stays a question.
- **Every fact kept:** numbers, clock times and money as digits; names, weekdays and months as in the source; technical terms (CRM, HDMI, database migration) kept in English, as natural Hinglish does.

`raw/` holds the selected sources (`sources_100.json`) and the lines in source order (`lines_100.py`). Mean 21.8 words per line, longest 34.
